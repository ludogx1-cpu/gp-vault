📖 How to use (click to expand)
  1. Upload a video or image — drag & drop anywhere on the panel below, or use the file picker.
  2. For videos: set your start/end times (or leave all at 0 for the whole clip). Use the ⏱️ buttons to grab the current playback position.
  3. (Optional) Remove background: tick the checkbox, then click anywhere on the Live Preview inside the chroma settings to pick the color to strip. Adjust tolerance until only the background disappears.
  4. (Optional) Apply transforms (rotate/flip) — these apply to both the live preview and the final output.
  5. Tick the output formats you want in section 2. Adjust scale/quality sliders — these take effect on the NEXT Process run, not the current preview.
  6. Click Process File. The animated preview in section 3 will auto-play when done — drag the scrubber to inspect individual frames.
  7. Download links appear in section 3 below the preview.
  8. (Optional) Click ✏️ Open in Sprite Editor to preview, delete or reorder frames and export a final sheet.

Turn video clips into game-ready sprite sheets

Golden Paw Animation Creation Tool converts a short video, such as footage you generated with an AI video model, recorded, or rendered, into the assets a game or website actually needs: a packed sprite sheet, a ZIP of individual frames, an animated GIF, and a TexturePacker-compatible JSON atlas that Phaser, PixiJS, Godot importers and many other engines read directly.

Everything happens locally in your browser using the Canvas API. Nothing is uploaded to a server, so it works with unreleased or confidential footage, and it is fast even for long clips.

Frequently asked questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The tool is free to use, supported by ads. There are no watermarks, no sign-up and no export limits.

Are my videos uploaded anywhere?

No. Your file is read and processed by your own browser. It is never sent to our servers, or anyone else's.

Which video formats work?

Anything your browser can play: MP4 (H.264), WebM and, in Safari, MOV. You can also drop in a single PNG or WebP image to key out its background.

What frame rate should I capture at?

12 fps is a good default for 2D game animation and keeps sheets small. Use 24 to 30 fps for smooth UI or web animations. Higher rates multiply the file size quickly.

Why is my sprite sheet so large?

Turn on Trim transparent padding, lower the Output Scale, use WebP, or capture fewer frames. The estimate box in section 2 updates as you change settings.

Can I use the exported assets commercially?

Yes, the output belongs to you. You are responsible for having the rights to the video you put in.
