How to use the Golden Paw Animation Creation Tool

Everything you need, from your first clip to a finished sprite sheet in your game. The tool runs in your browser, so nothing is uploaded.

Quick start

  1. Open the converter and drop in a short video (MP4 or WebM, 1 to 3 seconds is ideal).
  2. Trim it: play the video, pause where the motion starts and click ⏱️ Set Start. Pause where it is about to repeat and click ⏱️ Set End.
  3. Remove the background (optional): tick Remove Background and click the backdrop in the live preview.
  4. Click Process File. Check the animated preview, then download the sprite sheet and JSON.
  5. Polish it (optional): click ✏️ Open in Sprite Editor to delete stray frames, reorder, and export a final sheet.

Part 1: The converter, step by step

Step 1: Load your video

Use the file picker or drag a file onto the Input Settings panel. Anything your browser can play works: MP4 (H.264), WebM, and MOV in Safari. You can also load a single PNG or WebP image to remove its background.

Tip for great results: film or generate your subject on a flat, evenly lit, solid-colour background (green works best), with the camera locked off.

Step 2: Choose the section to capture

  • Start / End time: type exact times or use the ⏱️ buttons to copy the video's current position. Leave everything at 0 to capture the whole clip.
  • For a perfect loop: set the end one frame before the pose matches the start, so the first frame isn't shown twice.

Step 3: Pick a frame rate (Capture FPS)

Frames = seconds × FPS. 12 fps is a good default for games and keeps files small. Use 24 to 30 for very smooth motion. The Estimate box shows the frame count and approximate file size as you change it.

Step 4: Loop mode (optional)

  • None: frames as captured. Most engines loop back to frame 1 automatically.
  • Ping-pong: plays forward then backward. Great for breathing, idle bobs and tail wags.
  • Hold: repeats frame 1 at the end, for one-shot animations.
  • Bake loop frames: writes the extra frames into the sheet for engines that can only play forward.

Step 5: Rotate or flip (optional)

Use Rotate 90°, Flip Horizontal or Flip Vertical. For example, flip a right-facing run to get a left-facing one for free.

Step 6: Remove the background (optional)

  1. Tick Remove Background.
  2. Click the background in the Live Preview to sample its exact colour.
  3. Raise Tolerance until the backdrop disappears; lower it if holes appear in your subject.
  4. Set Edge feathering to around 10 to 20 for smooth edges (0 for pixel art).
  5. Uneven lighting? Set Match on to Colour only.
  6. Green fringe around the edges? Raise Spill suppression to about 60.

Want a solid background instead of transparency? Tick Add Solid Background Color. More detail in the green-screen guide.

Step 7: Choose your outputs

  • Sprite sheet: every frame in one image grid. This is what games use.
  • ZIP of individual frames: one image per frame.
  • Animated GIF: plays anywhere: Discord, socials, websites.
  • Atlas metadata (.json): tells game engines where each frame is. Keep it ticked.

Then fine-tune: WebP (small) or PNG (lossless, safest), Trim transparent padding (usually the biggest size saving), Output Scale, Quality and Sprite Columns (0 = auto, 1 = a single strip).

Step 8: Process and download

Click Process File. When it finishes, the preview plays automatically. Drag the scrubber to inspect single frames. Download buttons appear underneath.

Want a different size or layout? Change Scale, Quality or Columns and press 🔄 Rebuild. It reuses the frames instantly without decoding the video again.

Part 2: The sprite editor, step by step

Open it from the converter's ✏️ Open in Sprite Editor button, or go to the Sprite Editor and load any sprite sheet, including ones from other tools.

Step 1: Load a sheet

  • With a JSON atlas (recommended): choose the image and its .json, or drop both at once. Frames are cut exactly.
  • Without JSON: enter the number of columns and rows and click Slice by grid. Single-row strips are detected automatically.

Step 2: Preview the animation

Press ▶ Play and set the FPS. Choose Loop, Ping-pong or Play once, and zoom in. Tick Pixel art to keep pixels sharp. Use ← → to step one frame at a time.

Step 3: Fix the frames

  • Select: click a frame; Shift-click selects a range; Ctrl/⌘-click adds or removes one.
  • Delete blurry or duplicate frames (the usual cause of a stutter at the loop point).
  • Reorder: drag a frame, or use ← Move / Move →.
  • Duplicate a frame to hold a pose longer. Reverse flips the order.
  • Made a mistake? ↶ Undo (Ctrl+Z).

Step 4: Export

Set the columns, format and a file name, then click Export all frames, or Export selected only to split one sheet into several animations (for example idle, run, jump). You get a sheet image and a matching JSON.

Keyboard shortcuts

Space play/pause · ←/→ step · Delete delete selected · Ctrl+A select all · Ctrl+Z undo

Part 3: Use it in your game or website

  • Phaser / PixiJS: load the sheet and JSON as an atlas. See the copy-paste code.
  • Godot: AnimatedSprite2D → SpriteFrames → Add frames from sprite sheet, using the column and row counts shown under the preview.
  • Unity: set the texture's Sprite Mode to Multiple and slice by cell count.
  • Websites, socials, chat: use the animated GIF.

Install the app or extension

Install the tool as an app on your phone or computer. It then opens in its own window and works offline. Or add the browser extension to open it with one click. See Get the app.

Troubleshooting

My video won't load

Your browser can't decode that format. Convert it to MP4 (H.264) or WebM, or try Chrome or Edge. iPhone HEVC/MOV files usually only play in Safari.

Parts of my character disappear when I remove the background

Lower the tolerance, or pick a key colour that doesn't appear on the character. Re-shoot or re-generate on a green background if the subject is white or contains the backdrop colour.

There's a coloured halo around the edges

Raise Spill suppression and add a little Edge feathering.

The animation stutters when it loops

The last frame probably matches the first. Open the sheet in the Sprite Editor and delete the last frame.

The sprite sheet file is too big

Turn on Trim transparent padding, lower the Output Scale or FPS, or switch to WebP. Keep sheets under 4096×4096 px for mobile devices.

Transparency shows as black or white in my engine

Export as PNG instead of WebP.

"Open in Sprite Editor" didn't carry my sheet over

Some private-browsing modes block the storage it uses. Download the sheet and JSON, then load them in the editor manually.
